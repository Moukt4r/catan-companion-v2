# Sound packs

The app ships a fixed **cue vocabulary** — dice roll, progress by discipline,
barbarian advance and attack, world events, season changes, confirm — and a
**sound pack** decides how those cues are realised. Packs are interchangeable at
runtime and are a device preference, not part of a saved game.

## Available packs

| Pack     | Id         | Kind                     | Ships assets              |
| -------- | ---------- | ------------------------ | ------------------------- |
| Workshop | `workshop` | Synthesized per playback | No                        |
| Hearth   | `hearth`   | Pre-rendered one-shots   | Yes, `public/sfx/hearth/` |
| Silent   | `silent`   | Nothing plays            | No                        |

**Workshop is the default and must stay that way.** It is the only pack that
guarantees a first run with no network at all, and it is the fallback everything
else leans on.

## Why both synthesis and samples

They fail in opposite directions, so the app keeps both rather than picking a
winner:

- Synthesis never repeats itself. Dice roll number four hundred differs from the
  first, because the bounce times, pan positions and detune are drawn fresh. It
  also costs zero bytes. What it cannot do is sound like a real object; it sounds
  like a _very good model_ of one.
- Samples carry real material — grain, room, the messy detail that makes a die
  read as bone on wood. What they cannot do is vary. Played unchanged four
  hundred times, a sample becomes a tell.

So a sampled pack applies a small random playback-rate jitter (±18 cents) and
maps continuous cue parameters — barbarian distance, world-event impact — onto
rate and level. That buys back some variation without needing a clip per case.

## The fallback rule

This is the part worth not breaking:

> If a sampled asset is not loaded when a cue fires, the **synthesized** voice
> plays instead, and the asset is fetched for next time.

Consequences, all deliberate:

- a cue never waits on the network, so audio can never delay a roll;
- a broken or partial deployment degrades to Workshop, never to silence;
- a failed asset is remembered as failed, so a 404 costs one request rather than
  one request per roll;
- the first play of a rare cue may be synthesized even in a sampled pack. That is
  accepted: correct timing beats correct timbre.

Assets load lazily and per cue. Only dice, confirm and the three progress cues
are warmed up front, since those are what a table hits in the first minute.

## Adding or regenerating a pack

1. Produce one WAV per name in `sampleAssetNames`
   (`src/application/soundPacks.ts`). Names must match exactly.
2. Build the pack:

   ```bash
   node scripts/build-sound-pack.mjs <sourceDir> <packId>
   ```

   The script trims leading silence to the onset, fades the tail by 15 ms to
   avoid end clicks, peak-limits, and encodes to 48 kHz stereo Opus at 96 kbps
   VBR. It writes `public/sfx/<packId>/` plus a `manifest.json`, and fails if an
   asset is missing, effectively silent, still late after trimming, or if the
   pack exceeds its 512 KB budget.

3. Register the pack in `soundPacks` and add its id to the `soundPack` enum in
   `src/app/devicePreferences.ts`.
4. Record provenance below.

### Why the build script repairs instead of complaining

Rendered audio routinely carries a few tens of milliseconds of silence in front,
from a limiter's lookahead or a scheduler's first block. That silence is audible
lag when a die hits the table, and it is trivially fixable, so the script fixes
it rather than asking a human to.

Two measurement bugs were found while building this, both worth remembering:

- An earlier onset check used `/silence_start: (-?0(\.0+)?)\b/` to ask "does this
  file begin with silence?". That matches the `0` inside `silence_start: 0.907`,
  because there is a word boundary between the digit and the decimal point. Every
  correctly trimmed file then reported nearly a second of lead silence. The check
  now captures the whole number and compares numerically.
- The same class of error hit the earlier spectral analyser (see
  `scripts/analyse-audio.mjs`). **When a measurement says every single file is
  wrong in the same way, suspect the measurement first.**

Hearth was produced entirely by a text-to-audio model whose licence
(Apache-2.0) permits redistribution of its output, so no third-party recordings
are involved. Each clip's generating prompt and seed live in the staging
manifest kept out of the repo.

### The mid-presence gate, and why it exists

The first Hearth build shipped two cues Roger immediately called "bare skurr"
(just static): `barbarian-advance-far` and `season-autumn`. Everything I had
measured said they were fine. What the measurements missed:

| Metric | What it answers                   |
| ------ | --------------------------------- |
| `mid`  | share of energy in 300 Hz - 3 kHz |
| `hiss` | share of energy above 6 kHz       |

Both rejected cues scored `mid < 0.15`. **Every** cue he did not complain about
scored `mid > 0.20`. That band is roughly what a laptop, tablet or phone speaker
actually reproduces, so a clip can be perfectly clean, correctly timed and
normalised, and still arrive as static because all its content sits where the
speaker cannot go. A cue is now rejected if `mid < 0.15` or `hiss > 0.55`.

Applying the gate to the whole pack found four more cues heading the same way,
before anyone had to listen to them again.

### Continuous textures are noise

The deeper lesson is about briefs, not seeds. `season-autumn` asked for "dry
leaves rustling", `season-spring` for "light rain", `season-summer` for "warm
wind". Rustling, rain and wind **are** broadband noise. The model was right; the
brief was wrong, and no amount of reseeding fixes a cue whose target sound is
hiss.

The fix is to ask for _discrete events with transients_ that still read as the
same idea:

| Cue                      | Was                      | Now                                |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------ | ---------------------------------- |
| `season-autumn`          | leaves rustling          | footsteps crunching through leaves |
| `season-spring`          | light rain               | birdsong (tonal whistles)          |
| `season-summer`          | warm wind, then crickets | bees (tonal ~200-600 Hz wingbeat)  |
| `event-nature-setback`   | wind gust with rain      | a thunder crack                    |
| `barbarian-attack-board` | sub-bass impact          | heavy wooden gate slam             |

`season-summer` is worth singling out: the cricket rewrite scored `hiss 1.00`,
measurably **worse** than what it replaced, and was thrown away rather than
shipped. Cricket stridulation is high-frequency noise, so it failed the same way
wind did, just at the other end of the spectrum.

### One cue was not a generation problem at all

`barbarian-advance-far` was fixed without the GPU. Distance is a _filter_, not a
different performance, so the far drum is now derived from the near drum Roger
liked: low-pass for air absorption, high-pass to clear the sub-bass a small
speaker rattles on, plus a few decaying reflections. `mid` went from 0.14 to
0.65. Deriving a variant from a cue that already works is usually better than
asking a model for a second one.

Known model limitation: the DAC VAE is mono, so the shipped stereo files are
dual-mono. That is fine here — the cues are centred anyway and the engine adds
its own stereo room on top.

Rules:

- material must be CC0, public domain, or generated by a model whose licence
  permits redistribution of its output;
- model-generated audio records the model, and the prompt and seed where the
  tool exposes them;
- if provenance for a clip cannot be established, the clip does not ship.
